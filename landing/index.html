The form nobody told you about

Solo 401(k)s are wonderful — right up until your balance crosses $250,000. From that year on, the IRS requires an annual information return, Form 5500-EZ, due July 31. Miss it and penalties accrue daily. Here's why so many smart, diligent people get blindsided:

  • Your brokerage won't file it. Fidelity, Schwab, E*TRADE custody your assets — but the filing duty belongs to the plan administrator. For a solo 401(k), that's you. It's in your plan adoption agreement — the part nobody reads.
  • Your CPA probably doesn't handle it. Form 5500-EZ isn't an income-tax form and isn't in standard tax software, so many tax preparers decline it — or don't know it exists. Ask yours. If they handle it, use them. Seriously.
  • The penalty is $250 per day, up to $150,000 per return, per year. Source: IRS Form 5500-EZ instructions (irs.gov).
  • Missed past years too? The IRS has a penalty-relief program that caps the damage at $500 per return ($1,500 max) — but only if you fix it before they contact you. Rev. Proc. 2015-32 — our Amnesty Package walks you through it.

Questions people ask us

Do I even need to file Form 5500-EZ?

Only if your solo 401(k)'s total assets exceeded $250,000 at the end of the plan year — or if this is the plan's final year (you're closing it), in which case you must file regardless of balance. Use the free checker above; if you don't need to file, we'll tell you.

Why didn't my brokerage tell me about this?

Filing is the plan administrator's legal duty — and for a solo 401(k), the administrator is you. Brokerages custody assets; they don't administer your plan. It's buried in your adoption agreement.

Can't my CPA just do this?

Ask them — if they handle Form 5500-EZ, use them. Many decline because it isn't an income-tax form and isn't in standard tax software. That gap is why we exist.

Do you need my account logins or any access?

Never. You file on your own screen while we guide you. We never take custody of credentials, funds, or your return. That's by design — you shouldn't hand your retirement account to anyone.

What if I've missed previous years?

Don't panic, and don't wait: the IRS penalty-relief program (Rev. Proc. 2015-32) caps penalties at $500 per return / $1,500 total for most late filers — but only before the IRS contacts you. The Amnesty Package walks you through it step by step.

What if my situation is complicated?

Then we'll say so on the call, refund you in full, and point you toward an ERISA-savvy professional. We handle the straightforward 90% and we're honest about the rest.

What if we're running out of time before July 31?

Form 5558 can extend your deadline to October 15. Every session booked after July 25 includes the extension walkthrough free, as a safety net.
