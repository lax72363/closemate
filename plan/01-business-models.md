# 01 — Business Model Research & Scoring

Goal: choose on economics, not hype. 18 models evaluated against the 10 opportunity
filters (expensive problem, existing spend, provable ROI, automatable acquisition,
automatable delivery, recurring revenue, beatable competition, teen-accessible,
removable founder, paid-scalable).

## The 18 candidates

1. **Generic AI automation agency** — "we automate anything with AI" for SMBs.
2. **closemate: AI lead-response/ISA for real estate** — instant SMS response,
   qualification, nurture, booking for agents/teams. *(the incumbent concept in this repo)*
3. **Missed-call recovery + AI front desk for home services** — text-back, booking for
   plumbers/HVAC/tree service. *(the designated fallback)*
4. **Horizontal AI SaaS** — a general-purpose AI tool (writing, summarizing, etc.).
5. **Vertical AI SaaS (other niches)** — e.g., AI intake for law firms, AI quoting for
   contractors.
6. **B2B lead-gen / pay-per-appointment agency** — cold email infrastructure for clients.
7. **Done-for-you cold-email infrastructure** — domains, warmup, sequencing as a service.
8. **Productized creative service** — subscription design/video editing (AI-assisted).
9. **Digital products** — Notion templates, prompt packs, spreadsheets.
10. **Info products / courses** — teach a skill.
11. **Niche marketplace** — connect two sides of a small market.
12. **Software-enabled bookkeeping** — AI-assisted books for SMBs.
13. **AI accounting/financial workflow automation** — AR follow-up, invoice chasing bots.
14. **Compliance/admin automation** — licensing paperwork, permit filing for trades.
15. **High-ticket B2B consulting** — strategy/implementation retainers.
16. **Performance-based marketing (rev-share)** — get paid % of results.
17. **Ecommerce / dropshipping** — physical products via paid ads.
18. **Content/newsletter → ads/affiliate** — audience-first monetization.

### Why the losers lose (fast honesty)

- **Digital/info products (85, 73):** score high on cost and margin but fail filters
  1–3 — nobody's in *pain*; you're selling $19 items into infinite competition with no
  recurring revenue. Great pocket money, terrible $1M vehicle from a standing start
  with no audience.
- **Generic AI agency (75):** custom scoping per client = a job, not a system.
  Documented month-1 churn of 25–40% for poorly-onboarded white-label clients, and
  everyone with a GoHighLevel subscription is your competitor.
- **Horizontal SaaS / marketplace (76, 75):** 6–12 months to first dollar. Violates
  the speed-to-first-sale principle; you'd be building on savings you don't have.
- **Ecommerce (57):** capital-intensive, margin-poor, refund-heavy, ad-dependent.
  Worst fit for $200–400/week.
- **High-ticket consulting (67):** revenue = your hours. Explicitly banned by the
  "don't build me a job" rule.
- **AI accounting / compliance automation (95, 88):** genuinely good economics and
  *less* saturated — but slower first sale (trust-heavy buyers, longer cycles) and
  harder for a 17-year-old to be credible in. These are strong **year-3+ expansion
  candidates** once you have cash flow, not starting points.

### Why closemate wins (and it's close)

closemate (103) edges home services (102). The tie-breakers:

1. **You already started it** — this repo exists; momentum and founder interest are
   real economic inputs.
2. **The buyer's ROI math is bigger**: one closed deal ≈ $8–10k commission vs. a
   $300–3,000 plumbing job, so a $197/mo price is trivially justifiable.
3. **Proof-based selling is uniquely easy**: you can mystery-shop an agent's lead flow
   for free and show them *their own* failure — no cold pitch needed.
4. **Buyers are reachable at scale**: every agent's cell number and email is public by
   law (license records, MLS, brokerage sites). No gatekeepers.

The offsetting risk — solo-agent churn — is real and the plan handles it by moving to
teams/brokerages in year 2 (see plan/02). **If validation fails (kill-gate in
plan/04), home services is the pre-scored pivot: same tech stack, same playbook,
different phone lists.** That pivot costs you ~2 weeks, not a restart.

## Competition analysis (real names, real prices)

The demanded question isn't "is there competition" (yes) but "is there a gap" (also yes).

| Competitor | Price | Positioning | Weakness you exploit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structurely** | ~$499/mo + $0.12 per action credit (raised prices ~25% in April 2026) | AI ISA for teams; human-like long nurture | Priced out of the solo/small-team market; per-message fees feel like a taxi meter; sells via demo-call friction |
| **Ylopo (rAIya)** | ~$1,000+/mo incl. ad platform | All-in-one lead gen + AI follow-up | Forces you to buy their whole ad ecosystem; opaque pricing (hidden behind demo calls); overkill for agents who already have lead sources |
| **Follow Up Boss / Lofty / CINC** | $58–$900+/mo | CRMs with AI features bolted on | AI is an add-on, not the core; agent still has to drive it; CINC bundles expensive leads |
| **Smith.ai / human ISA services** | $255–$1,275/mo for 30–120 calls + overages | Human receptionists/hybrid | Per-call fees, business-hours bias, doesn't do 6-month SMS nurture |
| **GoHighLevel agencies (thousands)** | $97–$500/mo retail, wildly variable | White-labeled "AI follow-up" for any local biz | Generic horizontal setups, notoriously bad onboarding (25–40% month-1 churn), no vertical depth in RE scripts/compliance |
| **DIY (agent does nothing)** | $0 | The real #1 competitor | 63.5% of inbound leads never get any response; avg human response 15+ hours. Your mystery-shop proof attacks this directly |

**The gap:** flat-fee ($147–$297/mo, no per-message charges), real-estate-only depth
(scripts that know pre-approval vs. pre-qualification, listing vs. buyer leads,
Fair-Housing-safe language), 48-hour done-for-you setup, and proof-first sales. Nobody
owns "the affordable AI ISA for the 1.5M agents who aren't on a mega-team."

**Saturation check:** the *pitch* ("AI follow-up for realtors") is saturated on
Instagram; the *delivered product at this price with this onboarding* is not — the
incumbents keep raising prices (two raised in the same week of April 2026), which is
evidence demand exceeds supply at the top and the bottom tier is being abandoned, not
crowded.

**Acquisition channels incumbents use** (and you'll differentiate from): conference
booths, Facebook ads to agents, YouTube demo funnels, partnerships with lead vendors.
Your wedge — personalized mystery-shop proof — is too manual for a $499/mo VC-fed
company to bother with and exactly right for a founder whose time is cheaper than money.

## The 10 filters, applied to the winner

| Filter | closemate |
|---|---|
| 1. Expensive problem | Dead leads. Agent buying $500/mo of Zillow leads and converting 1–2% is burning $400+/mo |
| 2. Existing spend | Agents already pay for leads, CRMs, ISAs ($499–$1,275/mo documented above) |
| 3. Provable ROI | Response-time report + booked-appointments count, both machine-measurable |
| 4. Automatable acquisition | List-building, mystery-shopping, and outreach all scriptable (plan/03) |
| 5. Automatable delivery | It IS software; marginal delivery cost ≈ $15–30/client/mo |
| 6. Recurring | Monthly subscription, usage grows with client's lead volume |
| 7. Beatable competition | Gap at the $150–$300 flat-fee tier, documented above |
| 8. Teen-accessible | ~$100/mo tooling; no licenses required to sell software to agents (you're not brokering real estate — see plan/05) |
| 9. Founder-removable | Support, onboarding, and sales all delegable; product is code |
| 10. Paid-scalable | Agents are a precisely targetable ad audience (job title, MLS lists, FB groups) once organic proves the funnel |
