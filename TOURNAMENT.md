# Business Model Elimination Tournament

**Operator profile:** 17 years old, solo, Texas, $0 capital, smartphone only, no paid ads, target $10,000/mo in 6–9 months.

**Honesty note before anything else:** $10k/mo inside 9 months, solo, at 17, from a phone, is a top-1% outcome for *any* model. Nothing below changes that. What the tournament optimizes for is the model where the *median* outcome is still worth having ($2–4k/mo) and the $10k tail is genuinely reachable without capital, ads, or an adult's credentials. Any plan that promises $10k as the median is lying to you.

**One structural constraint that shapes everything:** at 17 you cannot open Stripe, PayPal, Gumroad, Shopify Payments, or Upwork accounts in your own name (all require 18+), and your contracts are voidable, so nobody serious will sign a long agreement with you. This quietly kills most "sell digital products to strangers on the internet" models and favors models where **adults pay you directly, prepaid, person-to-person** (Cash App/Venmo teen accounts with a parent sponsor, Zelle via a teen checking account, or cash). Local and relationship-based models survive this filter; anonymous e-commerce mostly doesn't.

## Round 4: Head-to-head bracket

**Play-in A — #85 job brokering vs. #51 AP cram products:** Brokering wins. It collects real checks in week one from an evergreen need (lawns grow in a recession); AP products are brutally seasonal (May exams), the market is saturated with free content (Heimler, Khan, r/APStudents), and a 17-year-old can't out-teach free. **#85 advances.**

**Play-in B — #15 Discord management vs. #98 Airbnb optimization:** Airbnb wins. Discord management clients are creators — flaky, broke, high churn, and finding them means competing with every other teen in every creator's DMs. Airbnb hosts are adults with income properties and a measurable problem (occupancy); one host often owns several listings. **#98 advances.**

**QF1 — #93 recruiting service vs. #98 Airbnb optimization:** Recruiting wins on access and trust. To an Airbnb host you are a random teen in their inbox; to a football parent at your school you are a known kid from the team with the QB's highlight reel as proof. Distribution is the whole game with no ad budget, and #93's distribution (team group chats, bleachers, booster clubs) is warm. **#93 advances.**

**QF2 — #28 bilingual retainer vs. #85 job brokering:** Bilingual retainer wins on margin durability and moat. Brokering has a structural flaw: once you hand a crew three jobs, the crew and customer can cut you out — no moat (scored 4), constant re-selling. The bilingual retainer is recurring by contract, and the Spanish-language wedge is a real filter competitors can't fake. **#28 advances.**

**QF3 — #31 delivery-menu optimization vs. #38 church media:** Delivery-menu wins on scale. Churches are wonderful clients with terrible budgets — a solo operator plateaus at $3–4k/mo (Scale 5). Restaurants' third-party-delivery revenue is real money they're visibly losing to bad listings, bad photos, and un-optimized menus; there are ~29,000 restaurants in DFW+Houston alone. **#31 advances.**

**QF4 — #27 generic review retainers vs. #76 HS sports media page:** Review retainers win on speed. The media page is audience-first: months of unpaid posting before sponsor #1 (Speed 4), and sponsorship checks are small. Review management invoices in week two. But note #27's weakness for later: "reputation management agency" has real course/agency saturation (Comp 5). **#27 advances.**

**SF1 — #93 recruiting service vs. #27 review retainers:** Recruiting wins. #27's fatal issue in this bracket is competition honesty: white-label reputation tools (NiceJob, Birdeye, Podium) sell directly to businesses for less than your retainer, and agencies bundle reviews for free. #93 has no software substitute — a parent cannot buy "personalized coach outreach" from a SaaS; the incumbent (NCSA) charges $3,000+ and is widely resented. **#93 to the final.**

**SF2 — #28 bilingual retainer vs. #31 delivery-menu optimization:** Bilingual retainer wins, narrowly. Delivery-menu optimization is closer to a one-time fix (Moat 6, recurrence weak — once the menu is fixed, why keep paying?), and it converts into a retainer only by becoming... a Google/social presence retainer, i.e., model #28. #28 is the same buyer with a recurring contract from day one. **#28 to the final; #31 takes third.**

**Top 3: #93 recruiting service · #28 bilingual local-biz retainer · #31 delivery-menu optimization.**

## Round 5: Stress tests

### #31 Delivery-app menu optimization (3rd)
- **What kills it:** recurrence. It's a $300–500 one-time fix that you must re-sell forever; converting to retainers turns it into model #28. Also DoorDash increasingly auto-optimizes listings with AI — the platform itself is eating the service.
- **Who's doing it:** restaurant consultants bundle it; a few agencies (e.g., delivery-ops shops serving chains). Almost nobody serves independents — genuinely low comp.
- **Why not saturated:** the buyer (independent restaurant owner) is hard to reach and skeptical; churn-heavy customer base. Unsexy = empty.
- **Month 1 from a phone:** audit 20 local restaurants' DoorDash listings (missing photos, no descriptions, no bundles), DM/walk in with a 1-page audit, close 2–3 at $300. Realistic ramp: $600 → $1.5k → $3k → plateau ~$4–5k/mo unless it becomes retainers. **Verdict: solid income, weak $10k path.**

### #28 Bilingual Google-presence retainer for Hispanic-owned TX businesses (2nd)
- **What kills it:** trust and sales cycle. Local business owners buy from people they trust; a 17-year-old closing $400/mo retainers takes reps, and Spanish fluency is mandatory (if you don't speak Spanish, this model is dead for you — that's a real conditional).
- **Who's doing it:** English-first GBP agencies (coursed, saturated) who ignore Spanish-dominant owners; a few bilingual freelancers, no systematized player in TX metros. The *bilingual* wedge is honestly low-comp.
- **Why not saturated:** the arbitrage exists precisely because agency bros can't speak Spanish and Hispanic-owned businesses (1 in 4 new TX businesses) are underserved. Durable gap.
- **Month 1 from a phone:** list 50 Hispanic-owned businesses with bad/absent Google profiles (taquerías, llanteras, salones, talleres), walk in or WhatsApp them in Spanish, fix one profile free for proof, close 2 at $300/mo. Ramp: $600 → $1.5k → $3k → $5k → $7k → $9–10k at ~25 clients × $400. Reaching 25 retainers solo in 9 months means closing ~3/month with churn — hard but arithmetic works. **Verdict: best pure recurring model; slower first dollar, sales-heavy.**

### #93 Recruiting reels + coach outreach (1st)
- **What kills it:** (a) sloppy work spreading through a school's parent network as fast as good work would; (b) seasonality if you stay football-only; (c) capacity — editing is hours, and you have school.
- **Who's doing it:** NCSA/CaptainU ($1,500–4,000, automated, widely resented — read the reviews), private recruiting consultants (ex-coaches, $2–5k, serve D1-track kids only), and local videographers (film games, don't do outreach). **Nobody serves the $400–800 tier** — the D2/D3/NAIA/juco family that needs film + emails, not a $3k package. No guru-course economy exists for "start a recruiting service."
- **Why hasn't it saturated:** the moat is access. You must be *inside* a high-school athletic ecosystem for warm distribution, and adults can't be. The people with access (students) don't run businesses. That's a structural gap that renews every year — and every year mints a new cohort of juniors (evergreen, recession-resistant: parents cut everything before they cut their kid's shot at a scholarship — they already pay $2–4k/yr for club sports).
- **Month 1 from a phone (it's July — perfect timing, summer camp/eval season, football starts late August):** get 2 teammates' Hudl logins, cut reels in CapCut, post before/afters, close first 3 paying families at founding price. Detail in the winner plan below.
- **Realistic ramp:** $900 → $2k → $4k (season peak) → $5.5k → $7k (Dec–Feb is *offer season panic* for seniors + juniors starting) → $8.5k → $10k around month 7–8 by adding volleyball/softball/baseball and raising prices. Median honest outcome: $3–5k/mo. Tail to $10k: real.

# FINAL VERDICT: #93 — Recruiting Media + Coach Outreach Service

**Why it beats #28 (bilingual retainer):** speed and access. #28's first dollar takes 3–6 weeks of cold walk-ins and its ramp needs ~25 simultaneous B2B retainers held by a minor whose contracts are legally voidable. #93's first dollar is 7–14 days away through people who already know you, the buyer (a parent) pays prepaid without a contract, and being 17 is an *asset* (peer access, insider credibility) instead of the liability it is in every B2B model. Same margin, warmer distribution, faster cash.

**Why it beats #31 (menu optimization):** recurrence and moat. #31 is a one-time fix with a platform (DoorDash AI) actively absorbing it. #93 renews structurally — a new junior class every single year, multi-sport expansion, sibling referrals — and its moat (relationships with families, coaches, and a testimonial wall of committed athletes) compounds; #31's doesn't.

**Why it's not secretly saturated:** the incumbents are priced 4–8x above you (NCSA $1.5–4k) and hated for being robotic; the cheap alternative is "dad does it," which produces 8-minute unwatchable reels and emails coaches ignore. There is no course industry teaching this, no YouTube-thumbnail economy around it, and the required asset — being embedded in a Texas high-school athletic community — cannot be bought by the 22-year-old agency crowd.

## The Winner's Playbook

### Exact offer + pricing
- **Core package — $499 "Recruit-Ready":** 3–4 min highlight reel cut from the athlete's Hudl/game film (CapCut, coach-standard: best 5 plays first, jersey spotlighting, no music-over-substance), a one-page recruiting profile (stats, measurables, GPA/test scores, schedule, coach contact), and a personalized email campaign to 30 college programs matched to the athlete's realistic level (D2/D3/NAIA/juco emphasis), sent from the athlete's email with you managing it.
- **Retainer upsell — $99/mo "In-Season":** new clips added within 48h of each game, monthly coach-outreach refresh, response tracking. This is the recurrence engine.
- **Founding price for clients 1–5: $299.** Never free after the first two proof athletes.
- Payment: Cash App or Venmo teen account (parent-sponsored — set this up day 1), prepaid in full. Parents pay; they're adults; no contract needed beyond a simple scope text.

### Target customer + where they hang out
**The buyer is the parent** (usually the mom) of a junior or sophomore athlete who is good-enough-for-college but not a 5-star — the kid Power-5 schools ignore and NCSA overcharges. Football first (this is Texas), then volleyball and softball (badly underserved — girls' sports parents are the most neglected and most grateful segment), then baseball/soccer/track.
Where they are: team parent group chats (GroupMe/WhatsApp), booster club meetings, the bleachers, team Instagram comment sections, and local "Sports Recruiting — [Your County]" Facebook groups. All reachable warm, from a phone, free.

### Day 1–30 execution plan (phone-only)
- **Days 1–2:** Parent-sponsored Cash App/Venmo teen account. Free CapCut + Canva accounts. Google Voice number. Instagram + TikTok handle (e.g., `@[YourTown]Recruits`). Google Form as intake sheet.
- **Days 3–7:** Recruit 2 proof athletes — teammates or friends, ideally one football, one volleyball, choose kids whose parents are loud in the group chats. Get their Hudl film. Cut both reels. Study what coaches actually want first (plenty of college coaches have posted free guidance; follow 20 college position coaches on Twitter/X and read how they talk about film).
- **Days 8–12:** Build each proof athlete's profile page in Canva, write the coach-email template (short, film link in first line, measurables, transcript offer), send both athletes' campaigns to 30 matched schools each. Post both reels tagged to the athletes — they will reshare, and their parents will reshare. This is your entire marketing engine.
- **Days 13–18:** The founding offer. Text the 10 parents you know best: "I made [proof athlete]'s recruiting film and sent it to 30 college coaches — first responses already coming in. I'm taking 5 athletes at $299 before the season starts, then it's $499." **First dollar target: day 14–16.**
- **Days 19–30:** Deliver clients 1–5 fast (48–72h turnaround each — batch edit at night). Ask every parent for one referral and one group-chat mention on delivery. Post every before/after. Collect any coach reply screenshots (with permission) — these are your testimonial wall. Raise to $499 the moment the fifth founding slot fills.

### First-dollar strategy
The two free proof reels are the whole strategy: they convert your existing locker-room relationships into visible, reshared evidence inside the exact group chats where buyers live. Fastest path: pick proof athletes whose parents are group-chat superconnectors. Payment #1 should arrive within 48 hours of the founding-offer text, roughly day 14.

### The 3 most likely failure points
1. **Quality debt.** One rushed reel with the wrong plays first, circulated in a parent group chat, poisons your only distribution channel. *Avoid:* a fixed checklist per reel (best 5 plays in first 60s, jersey spotlight on every play, working link tested on mobile), and cap intake at what you can deliver in 72h — turn people away publicly ("booked until the 15th"), which also builds scarcity.
2. **Football-only seasonality.** If you're football-only, revenue craters in spring and the ramp dies at month 5. *Avoid:* onboard volleyball by month 3 and softball/baseball by month 6; girls' sports are the least served and most referral-dense. Dec–Feb is not dead time — it's senior panic + junior campaign season; message accordingly.
